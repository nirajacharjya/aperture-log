Why I Built a Personal Website Instead of Chasing Social Media Algorithms

Niraj Acharjya
Jul 25, 20265 min read

Building something that stays yours, one commit at a time.

A few years ago, if someone had asked me where I wanted to share my work, I would have probably answered "Instagram" or "YouTube." Today, my answer is different. I built my own website.

Not because I think social media is bad. In fact, I still believe it has helped countless creators reach millions of people. But somewhere along the way, I realized I didn't want my entire creative journey to depend on an algorithm.

Algorithms change. Platforms rise and disappear. But a personal website remains yours.

More Than Likes

One thing I noticed while using social media was how quickly everything disappeared. You upload a photo. Someone scrolls past it in two seconds. A week later, it's buried under thousands of newer posts. It almost feels like your work has an expiration date.

I wanted a place where every photograph, every article, every project, and every lesson I learned would still be easy to find months or even years later — a place that grows instead of disappearing.

Building Something Instead of Just Posting

Creating Aperture Log has been completely different from posting on social media. Instead of thinking about filters and hashtags, I spend my time writing code, improving performance, fixing bugs, designing pages, and learning technologies I had never touched before.

Every small improvement feels rewarding. Sometimes nobody notices that a page loads faster. Nobody notices that I spent hours fixing a layout issue that only appeared on mobile devices. Nobody notices the caching changes or the tiny performance optimizations. But I notice them, and every one of those improvements teaches me something new.

Learning in Public

I'm not an expert. I'm still a computer science student. I'm still learning web development. I still make mistakes almost every day. Sometimes I write code that completely breaks my website. Sometimes I spend hours searching for a bug that turns out to be a missing semicolon or a typo.

But that's exactly why I wanted this website. Instead of pretending to know everything, I can document what I'm learning. Maybe one day someone else who is just starting out will read one of these posts and realize they're not the only one figuring things out.

Owning My Work

When you publish on your own website, you're building something that belongs to you. The articles. The photographs. The projects. The design. The experience. Everything reflects your own ideas instead of fitting into someone else's platform.

That feeling is hard to describe, but it's incredibly motivating.

This Is Only the Beginning

Aperture Log is still far from complete. There are features I haven't built yet. There are articles I still want to write. There are photographs waiting to be taken. And there are countless things I still need to learn.

But that's okay. This website isn't meant to be perfect. It's meant to grow alongside me.

Maybe one day this site will become something much bigger than I imagined. Or maybe it won't. Either way, years from now I'll be able to look back at these posts and remember exactly where my journey started. And honestly, I think that's worth far more than chasing an algorithm.

Thanks for reading. Whether you found this article through a search engine or by chance, I hope it encourages you to create something that truly belongs to you.

Build slowly, keep learning, and don't be afraid to share your journey.
